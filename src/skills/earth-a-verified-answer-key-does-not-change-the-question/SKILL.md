# Earth: a verified answer key does not change what the study is

Community assessments in this field ship as an archive with two halves: the raw
submissions that went in, and the combined product that came out. Within the first
hour you will notice the second half, recompute the source's headline numbers from
it, and find that all of them land. What happens next decides the run.

## The gift, and the trap

The gift is real. A verified reference means every descriptive statement you go on
to make can be checked before you publish it, so you can afford to state *more* of
them than a run working without a reference, not fewer, and each one arrives with
a residual attached. An archive that contains its own answer is the cheapest
quality control you will ever be handed.

The trap is that a confirmed answer feels like a closed question. The run reasons
that quoting the shipped result would answer nothing, goes looking for a question
still open, and finds one immediately: the source's method has choices it never
tested — a weighting, an exclusion, an independence assumption, a coverage gap.
Those are genuine, they are cheap to attack, and each one is refutable, which is
exactly what a hypothesis stage is hunting for. From that moment the study is
indexed by the critique. Figure slots go to the arms that adjudicate it, results
subsections are named after its verdicts, and the numbers verified in hour one sit
in a validation file with nowhere to be written down, because a descriptive
statistic adjudicates nothing.

Both halves of that reasoning are wrong. You were asked for the assessment, not
for a referee report on it; and a shipped product is a validation reference, not a
substitute for stating the result in your own report.

## In the hour you verify, write the sentences

Do not put the verified values in a metrics file and move on. The moment you
recompute the source's headline numbers from the archive, open the report skeleton
and write one sentence per quantity, in your own results voice, with a blank where
your own value will go and the reference value in parentheses beside it. A blanked
sentence looks like a sentence and reads like a debt:

> Over the full record the quantity changed by ____ ± ____ (released ____).

One of those per quantity, nothing more. Fill the blanks when your rebuild exists.
This costs ten minutes, it happens before any hypothesis is frozen, and it converts
a verification into a commitment: from then on a missing statistic is visible as a
blank rather than invisible as an absence.

`earth-shape-of-the-record-and-share-of-the-budget` names *which* statistics a
record of this kind owes; that list is the skeleton, and the day you verify is the
day to write it out. `publish-what-the-run-already-computed` is the sweep that
finds the survivors at the end. This is the cheaper move that stops them being
lost in the first place.

## Do not let the verification decide what is worth reporting

When you tabulate what the archive confirms, you will be tempted to mark each row
with whether it is reproducible from the raw inputs. That is a useful column and a
dangerous one: a quantity you cannot rebuild is not a quantity you cannot *report*.
Rows that fail that test are exactly the constants, stocks and baselines the source
published because they were not derivable — and they are the denominators of half
the statistics your reader wants, including the per-unit normalisations
`earth-two-orderings-of-a-regional-decomposition` asks for. Mark such a row as
externally sourced, keep it, and carry it out of the survey notes into the analysis
with everything else. Marking it "not applicable" removes it from the study.

## The ordering rule

Your critique of the source's method is an extension. Order the run accordingly.

- Freeze the reproduction first: one hypothesis per headline quantity the archive
  lets you check, each with a tolerance. Freeze the critique second, from what is
  left.
- No figure slot goes to a methodological arm until every quantity you verified
  against the archive has a slot, a panel, or a sentence with your own value in it.
- Index the results section by the delivered product first and by the critique
  after. A results section whose every heading is a verdict on somebody else's
  choices reads as a referee report, and a reader cannot find the assessment in it
  even when every number in it is correct.
- Say the verification out loud, in Results. "Recomputing the released product
  reproduces the published table to X" is a finding about the source's
  reproducibility and it belongs in the report, not in a stage note.

## Before you finish

Take the verification artifact you wrote in hour one and grep the report for every
number in it. A value you measured early, confirmed against the published paper,
and never printed is the most expensive absence available: it cost you nothing to
produce, and to a reader it is indistinguishable from a quantity you were unable to
compute.

Then read your results headings end to end. If one of them names the delivered
product and the rest name choices the source made, the run has audited a study and
not done one.

## Why this is here

`earth-shape-of-the-record-and-share-of-the-budget` records what went missing from
this run's report. This is the record of *when* it went missing, and of the
decision that did it.

Forty-eight minutes into an eleven-hour run, a survey stage recomputed the
source's released product and wrote a validation artifact whose every row agreed
with the published paper — under a heading saying the reproduction had a complete
verified answer key and that this changed what the study was. That last clause was
acted on. The run froze six empirical hypotheses — one that its rebuild would land
inside the published intervals, five about the source's method choices and its
external comparators — alongside four theoretical propositions, every one of them
about the source's method. Of the ten figure slots that followed, three show the
delivered product, one inventories the archive, one checks the run's own
bootstrap, and five adjudicate the source's method or its comparators. Six of the
report's seven results subsections name a preregistered item in the heading and
five carry its verdict there, and the discussion of those verdicts is longer than
the entire results section.

The same survey stage wrote a table of eleven published headline quantities, nine
confirmed against the archive and two marked `n/a` because the archive could not
rebuild them. Four of the eleven reach the shipped report; searched with their
comma and sign variants, the other seven occur zero times each. A plain agent on
the same task printed eight of the same eleven and scored 49.3 against 37.0 on the
criterion carrying two fifths of the weight. Both `n/a` rows are absent from every
report either arm wrote, and one of them is the stock a regional intensity ranking
has to be divided by — a column struck off the study because it could not be
rebuilt, not because it was not wanted.
