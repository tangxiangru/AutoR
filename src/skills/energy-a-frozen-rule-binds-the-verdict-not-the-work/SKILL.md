# A frozen rule binds the verdict. It does not bind the work.

Freezing a decision rule before the data exists is the right habit, and it
protects exactly one thing: the mapping from a statistic to a verdict. It says
nothing about which statistics you may compute, which populations you may cost,
which cases you may add, or which numbers reach the report. Runs that take the
discipline seriously routinely extend it past its subject, and the extension is
expensive — it turns a rule written to stop you fooling yourself into a rule
that stops you finishing the task.

Two questions get fused, and they have different owners. *Would moving the
population change my verdict?* Yes, which is why the verdict stays on the frozen
population. *Would moving the population change the answer to the task?* That is
not a question about your protocol. It has a numeric answer, and the reader is
asking it.

## Both go in, as two rows

One table. The frozen population with its frozen verdict, and the extended
population with the same statistic beside it, labelled exploratory, with the
reason the extension exists stated before its value is quoted — *the diagnostic
in §5 says the published minimum sits on a coastline our sample never reaches,
so we costed coastal cells through the identical chain.* A reader can discount a
labelled row. A reader cannot recover a row that is not there, and a verdict
with no second row reads as the only thing you know.

The extension is a run, not a paragraph, so it has to happen while compute is
open. The moment a diagnostic names the thing that would close your gap, that
naming is a work item with a script attached. If the missing case is a
coordinate, a configuration, a threshold value or a held-out subgroup, generate
it and cost it. Declining because it is outside the frozen population answers a
procedural question nobody asked and leaves the substantive one open.

## The sentences that mark a withheld number

At analysis, and again on the draft, search your own notes, captions and prose
for these:

- "outside the preregistered population"
- "the frozen rule does not classify"
- "selecting the population after seeing the count"
- "we report the frozen verdict, because"
- "changes no verdict"

Every hit is a place where a number was computed, or was one script away, and
was withheld on procedural grounds. For each, produce the number and print it
beside the frozen one. *Changes no verdict* is a statement about your rule; if
the thing it describes moves a quantity the task asked for, say by how much.

## When the frozen rules land on their fallbacks

If several rules collapse into degenerate branches at once — a count that is
zero everywhere, a classification with one class, a difference whose terms
cancel identically — the freeze is telling you it was written against a quantity
your run has no variance in. Say that once, in a sentence. Then answer the
underlying question with a quantity that does vary: the distance to the
threshold per entity, and the value of the input at which the leaders cross.
`energy-publish-the-ordering-when-the-level-fails` owns the shape of that
answer; this skill is about not letting a frozen rule be the reason you never
reach it.

## A deliverable was never inside the freeze

Put the task's list of outputs beside your frozen rule set. Anything the task
named that no rule adjudicates is not governed by the freeze at all: there is no
verdict to protect, so withholding it buys nothing. Most of what gets suppressed
this way is in exactly that category — a map, a ranked list, a per-entity
margin — none of which any hypothesis was ever going to decide.

When you do extend, amend openly: one dated line in the ledger naming what you
saw and what you added. An empty amendment ledger at the end of a long run is
evidence about how much the run could move, not about how rigorous it was.

## Why this is here

agent's 52.0. The run held the fix and declined it in writing. Its own §5.3
reports that the seven Atlantic coordinates in the supplied file close 78.2% of
